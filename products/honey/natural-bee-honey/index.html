About Natural Bee Honey

Our honey is raw, mature and unprocessed

Bees collect a dilute sugary solution called nectar and pollen from flower. Nectar contains 60 to 80% moisture.

If you look at the honeycomb, you will see that some cells are partially filled with liquid. This visible liquid is immature honey and not the final product. It contains higher percentage of water, hence shelf life is not long, yeast and bacteria present in surrounding will soon spoil it.

Worker bees consume nectar and produce honey in several days using complex biological and physical process. Final mature honey contains about 16 to 20% moisture. Bees store these in hexagonal cells and cover the opening with a wax cap. Fully sealed honey comb contains ripe and mature honey.

This indeed is the raw honey that we collect. We harvest this from bee hives always in personal presence and bottle it raw. It is wholesome with all natural ingredient, enzyme, hormones intact.

What is processed honey

Majority of the honey available in the market are processed honey. Why?

A single commercial bee farm maintains from few dozens to several hundreds of bee boxes. Physically checking each box and to find out if all the cells are capped is a labour intensive and time consuming task. Short flowering season and limited manpower do not allow such a proposition. Hence, bee keepers do not wait for honey comb to be fully capped.

Harvesting is done at varying degree of maturity, say, some are 100% sealed and some are roughly 60 to 80% sealed. They collect all the honeycombs from all the boxes in a single go and extract honey. Honey extracted in this way is high in moisture content and not ripe. So the shortcut solution adopted universally is to heat or boil it to get rid of excess moisture. In addition, fine filtration is done to remove pollen, a beneficial ingredient. Pollen acts as nucleus for crystallization, hence commercial honey supplier removes it by filtration.

Processed honey – Heat destroys nutrients

It is a general natural phenomenon that high temperature degrades delicate ingredients. Processing involves heating to 55 to 80°C. We believe that processed honey loses some of its health benefit.

Benefits of honey

Antioxidant Activity: Honey contains phenolic acids and flavonoids (such as chrysin, luteolin, and kaempferol) that neutralize harmful free radicals and reduce cellular oxidative stress.

(Ref.: Research article “Health benefits of honey: A critical review on the homology of medicine and food in traditional and modern contexts” published in ‘Health benefits of honey: A critical review on the homology of medicine and food in traditional and modern contexts’, Published in Apr-2025, Vol 12 Issue 2)

Cardiovascular and Metabolic Support: Human and animal trials suggest that natural honey intake may help improve lipid profiles by reducing total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein, and triglycerides, while potentially elevating high-density lipoprotein. (Ref. : “A Comprehensive Review of the Effect of Honey on Human Health” published in ‘Nutrients’ dated July 2023)

Cough Suppression: Research indicates that honey can effectively soothe and suppress nocturnal coughing in children over one year of age, often performing comparably to over-the-counter cough suppressants.

Gastrointestinal and Prebiotic Support: Oligosaccharides present in honey act as prebiotics, promoting the growth of beneficial gut microbiota, while secondary studies point to mild anti-inflammatory effects in the digestive tract.

Is farm honey inferior

No, it is not inferior. Bees collect nectar and pollen from flower. Whether it is wild bee or bees kept in farmer’s box, honey making process is same.

It may be noted that concentrated nectar is NOT honey. Honey is a liquid generated by worker bees within its belly. Nectar and pollen are only raw material.

The owner of Noblemens personally witnessed a phenomenon hence hitherto unknown to many bee experts. In 1997 or 1998 he and his family resided in a high rise building in Anushaktinagar, Trombay. A swarm of Apis dorsata (giant bee, or rock bee) settled outside his 12th floor kitchen window and started a colony.

He slightly opened the glass panel and placed sugar syrup using a stick. It was observed that bees were not much interested in it however a section of bees accepted it. Later he saw visually from a close proximity of about 25 to 30 inch that bees repeatedly regurgitate it till a small droplet of about 1 to 2 mm diameter appeared in their mouth, then after few seconds drink it back.

Bees did it repeatedly while quietly resting on hive. It is believed that one reason for doing it to evaporate excess water. This also confirms that formation of honey takes place inside the belly of bees.

Jungle honey

Wild honey itself isn't the problem—It is the time of collection and method of extraction that is objectionable. Wild honeycombs are simply squeezed by hand, it introduces impurities and hygiene risks. Another concern – harvesting is done in a single visit to jungle, combs are collected irrespective of maturity. These traditional methods turn a premium natural resource into a second-grade product. How to Use

For daily use, mix 1–2 level teaspoons of honey with a cup of normal-temperature water and drink it on a relatively empty stomach.

For cough: Take 1–2 level teaspoons of honey without water.

For instant energy: Take 2 level teaspoons of honey with or without water at any time.

With milk: Mix 2 level teaspoons of honey in a cup of milk.

With hot tea: We do not recommend adding honey to very hot tea if you want to avoid unnecessary heat exposure.

Important: Honey should not be given to children below 1 year of age.
